About Me

Hi, I'm Katie! I'm currently pursuing my M.S. in Computer Science at USC and interning at Google this summer. I'm interested in building AI systems that are reliable, useful, and grounded in the needs of the people who use them. My work spans software engineering, machine learning, and human-centered AI, with a growing research focus on how increasingly personalized systems model and interact with people, and how those interactions can reinforce gender bias and unequal power dynamics.

Outside of work: weightlifting · hiking · reading · road trips · exploring new places

How I Got Here

Starting with people

My path into computer science started with human-centered design. I studied Human-Centered Design & Engineering at the University of Washington, where I learned how to conduct user research, design accessible systems, and think carefully about the ways technology shapes people's experiences.

Moving beneath the interface

After graduating, I worked in UX design, but I gradually became more interested in the problems happening underneath the interface. A thoughtful design can make a product easier to use, but it cannot fix a biased model, an unreliable AI system, or a backend that does not work as intended. I wanted to understand and work on those deeper technical layers, which led me to pursue an M.S. in Computer Science at USC.

Where I'm headed

My HCI background still shapes how I approach technical work. I care about building systems that work well in practice (and are reliable, scalable, and useful), but I also want to understand who they work for, how they fail, and whom those failures affect. Going forward, I hope to continue growing as an engineer while pursuing research on gendered harms in increasingly personalized AI systems, bringing technical depth and human-centered thinking to both.
